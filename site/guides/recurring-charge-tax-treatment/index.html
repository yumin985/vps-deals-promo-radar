VPS deals reader question

How do I verify the recurring VPS charge when tax is separate?

Answer: record the provider’s billing term and recurring amount separately from the checkout tax line. A tax notice can show that tax may apply, but it does not prove the recurring VPS charge. Keep the plan, term, currency, subtotal, tax, and total together; leave any unpublished value unknown.

What the public provider pages establish

Hostinger’s public VPS page says plans are paid upfront and describes a monthly rate as the total plan price divided by the plan months. That is evidence about payment presentation, not a universal recurring renewal amount. IONOS’s public cheap VPS page says state and local sales taxes may apply. Neither public page supplies one customer-specific tax amount for every checkout location.

Capture the checkout fields in order

  1. Plan and term: copy the exact plan name and the period covered by the charge.
  2. Recurring amount: record the provider’s explicit renewal or recurring figure, if published.
  3. Subtotal: record the pre-tax amount in the same currency.
  4. Tax: copy the exact tax line or rate shown for the checkout location.
  5. Total: record what is charged now and check that it covers the same term.

If the tax line changes the currency, term, or subtotal, stop before ordering and reconcile those fields. Do not derive a renewal amount by subtracting an estimated tax or by treating a display “/month” figure as proof of monthly billing.

What remains unknown

For this public-source check, the buyer-specific tax amount, checkout subtotal, final total, and any recurring amount not explicitly published by the provider remain unknown. That is a factual state, not a gap to fill with an estimate.

Verification rule: accept a recurring-charge claim only when the provider names the term and amount; keep tax and total as separate evidence from the same checkout.
